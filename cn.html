Meta-analysis Mentorship

Learn meta-analysis by doing your own

You work on your own title: bring your topic, or I suggest a meta-analysis title in your field. Then, stage by stage, you do every part of the work yourself, and I guide and check each step. You finish as the first author of a manuscript ready for a journal, with the skills to lead the next one on your own.

Learning by doingEverything you learn, you apply the same week to your own project.

I have trained many researchers in medicine, psychology, nursing and other health sciences. Many of them are now among Iran's leading researchers, and several have been listed among the World's Top 2% Scientists.

Who it's for

MedicinePsychologyNursing & midwiferyPublic health & epidemiologyDentistry & pharmacyOther health sciences

Format

  • One-to-one or in a small group
  • Online or in person
  • No statistics background needed
  • Moderate English or Persian is enough
  • You are the first author of your paper

Also part of the training

  • Reference management with EndNote
  • Using AI tools at each stage of research
  • Working with Claude to read, write and revise faster, within journals' AI policies
  1. 01

    Your own research title

    You choose your own topic, or I suggest a meta-analysis title in your field. We check that there is a real research gap and enough studies, then turn it into a focused PICO question.

    You finish withA focused question and protocol
  2. 02

    Search strategy

    You build search strings with keywords, MeSH terms and Boolean operators, and run them in PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Embase and national databases.

    You finish withA documented search for every database
  3. 03

    Study selection

    You screen titles, abstracts and full texts against your inclusion and exclusion criteria, and record every decision.

    You finish withA PRISMA flow diagram
  4. 04

    Data extraction

    You design an extraction form and extract sample sizes, effect sizes and study characteristics from each included study.

    You finish withA clean extraction sheet, ready for analysis
  5. 05

    Quality assessment

    You assess risk of bias with the right tool for your designs, such as Newcastle–Ottawa, Cochrane RoB 2, JBI or QUADAS-2.

    You finish withA quality-assessment table
  6. 06

    Data analysis

    You run the meta-analysis yourself in Stata or R: pooling, heterogeneity, subgroup analysis, meta-regression and publication bias.

    You finish withForest and funnel plots and all results
  7. 07

    Reporting the results

    You interpret and write up the results following PRISMA 2020, with the tables and figures journals expect.

    You finish withA results section with figures
  8. 08

    Manuscript and submission

    You write the introduction and discussion, manage references in EndNote, choose the right journal, prepare the cover letter and learn to respond to reviewers.

    You finish withA manuscript submitted to a journal
Statistical consulting

When you need the statistics done for you

Take one piece or the whole pipeline, from the first sample-size calculation to the last reviewer response.

Study design & sample size

Clinical trials, cohort, case-control and cross-sectional designs; power and sample-size calculations; statistical analysis plans.

Statistical analysis

From descriptive statistics to multivariable models, with re-runnable syntax in SPSS, Stata, R, SAS or Python.

Systematic review & meta-analysisMost requested

Fixed- and random-effects and network meta-analysis, heterogeneity, subgroup analysis and meta-regression, publication bias, forest and funnel plots, diagnostic-accuracy meta-analysis.

Survival & longitudinal analysis

Kaplan–Meier, Cox models with time-dependent covariates, Weibull/AFT models, competing risks, semi-Markov multistate models, mixed models and GEE.

Methods & Results writing

Statistical sections written to your target journal's style and reporting guidelines (CONSORT, STROBE, PRISMA).

Reviewer-response support

Additional analyses and clear replies to statistical comments during revision.

Thesis & research advisory

Statistical advisor for MSc and PhD students and research teams, from proposal to defence.

Workshops for universities & teams

Hands-on workshops in biostatistics, Stata/R, survival analysis and meta-analysis, online or on campus.

About

Kourosh Sayehmiri, PhD

Professor of Biostatistics and Head of the Department of Biostatistics at Ilam University of Medical Sciences. FAQ

Common questions

Do I need a statistics background for the mentorship?

No. Each stage starts by teaching the method, and then you apply it to your own project. A moderate level of English or Persian is enough to follow the training.

How long does the mentorship take?

It depends on the size of your topic and how much time you can give each week. We agree a schedule at the start.

Whose name goes on the paper?

Yours. The paper is your own work and you are its first author. Any other authors are agreed at the start, following ICMJE criteria.

How much does it cost?

Mentorship and consulting projects are quoted individually. You receive the fee before any work begins.

Is my data kept confidential?

Yes. Your data are used only for your project and are not shared with anyone. Anonymised data are preferred.

Which software will you use?

Whatever suits your team: SPSS, Stata, R, SAS or Python. You receive the syntax so every analysis can be re-run.

Can you help after I submit my paper?

Yes. I support additional analyses and responses to statistical reviewer comments during revision.

Do you run workshops for institutions?

Yes. Biostatistics, survival analysis, Stata/R and systematic review & meta-analysis workshops, online or in person.
